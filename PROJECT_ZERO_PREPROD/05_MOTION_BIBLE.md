# PROJECT ZERO — MOTION BIBLE (DOCUMENT 5)

> **Status:** Pre-Production (Planning Only) — **No code.**

---

## 0. Document Intent
Define motion rules for PROJECT ZERO across:
- opacity
- blur
- scale
- rotation
- depth
- fog
- lighting
- particles
- geometry
- text
- camera
- scene transitions

Motion must resemble a Christopher Nolan opening sequence: precise, restrained, rhythm with breathing moments.

---

## 1. Global Motion Laws
1. Nothing moves quickly.
2. No bounces.
3. No random spins.
4. Motion always clarifies story.
5. Every scene alternates intensity: slow → pause → reveal → calm.
6. Typography resolves with light/fog emergence—not slide-in.
7. Transitions are transformations of the world.
8. Particles may accelerate only during emotion peaks.
9. Fog density changes must align with contrast reduction rules.
10. Signature object evolves to absorb the world.

---

## 2. Easing & Timing Philosophy
### 2.1 Duration bands
Use consistent timing bands across the experience:
- Micro reveals (glow settle): 0.4–0.9s
- Medium transformations (layer dissolve): 1.0–2.2s
- Scene cuts (world change): 2.2–4.0s
- Breathing holds (silence moments): 2.0–6.0s

### 2.2 Easing types (conceptual)
- Ease-out primary for reveals
- Soft ease-in for camera pushes
- Avoid elastic easing

---

## 3. Motion Tokens (Conceptual)
Define the *meaning* of motion parameters.

### 3.1 Opacity motion
- Opacity changes should be accompanied by either:
  - fog density change, or
  - light intensity shift, or
  - DOF change

Forbidden:
- opacity-only fades that look like web transitions.

### 3.2 Blur motion
- Blur increases with mystery/conflict.
- Blur decreases smoothly into reveal.
- Text blur → sharp is the preferred typography behavior.

### 3.3 Scale motion
- Scale changes are rare.
- Used for:
  - title climax emphasis
  - signature object evolution

### 3.4 Rotation motion
- Rotation is minimal.
- No freeform rotations.
- If used, it must represent reorientation of the system.

### 3.5 Depth motion
- Depth cues must be created by:
  - parallax
  - fog boundaries
  - lighting separation

---

## 4. Fog & Transition Motion Grammar
### 4.1 Fog as timeline
Fog density behaves like the scene’s emotional meter.
- Mystery: low-to-medium fog, contrast low
- Conflict: medium fog with sharper local highlights
- Transformation: fog spikes while signature absorbs fragments
- Resolution: minimal fog, clarity returns

### 4.2 Transition recipes
Use these as reusable transition compositions:
1. **Absorb + Rebuild:** signature object pulls fragments inward; fog thins to reveal new geometry.
2. **Dissolve + Reveal:** mid layer dissolves into fog; title emerges behind boundary.
3. **Collapse + Passage:** geometry collapses into particles; camera passes through particle field into next sky gradient.

---

## 5. Lighting Motion Rules
### 5.1 Bloom motion
Bloom never flickers rapidly.
- Bloom blooms during reveals (title climax)
- Bloom subsides during calm holds

### 5.2 Light rays / God rays
Use rarely:
- only during discovery and transformation climax.

### 5.3 Mouse lighting behavior
Mouse changes should be:
- smooth
- delayed slightly (film-like inertia)
- never jumpy

---

## 6. Particle Motion Rules
### 6.1 Particle density
- Calm: sparse, stable drift
- Mystery: slightly denser, slower drift
- Conflict: denser near failure zones
- Reveal: particles converge into lines or into signature absorption

### 6.2 Particle “meaning”
Particles must represent something:
- clause fragments
- unstable pathways
- message chaos
- eligibility connectivity
- system glitches
- memory shards

Forbidden:
- generic glitter

---

## 7. Geometry Motion Rules
Geometry should “act” like a system:
- assembly (reliability)
- disassembly (conflict)
- reorientation (transformation)

No chaotic physics.

---

## 8. Text Motion Rules (Monumental Typography)
### 8.1 Preferred emergence method
- fog-to-ink convergence
- light sweep across letters
- DOF sharpening as text becomes readable

### 8.2 Forbidden text behaviors
- typing effect
- random flicker
- slide-in from edges

---

## 9. Camera Motion Rules (from Document 3)
Camera motion inherits the shot types.
In addition, define how camera participates in motion events:
- During emotional reveals: camera settles, focus resolves.
- During transitions: camera passes through fog boundary or behind signature.
