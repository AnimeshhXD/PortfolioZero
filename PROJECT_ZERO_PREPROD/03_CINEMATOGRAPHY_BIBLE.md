# PROJECT ZERO — CINEMATOGRAPHY BIBLE (DOCUMENT 3)

> **Status:** Pre-Production (Planning Only) — **No code.**

---

## 0. Document Intent
This document defines the PROJECT ZERO camera language so every scene feels like a film shot.

It is a **camera contract**: camera movement + lens philosophy + focus behavior + pacing rules.

---

## 1. Camera Philosophy (Emotion-first)
Camera is not motion decoration. Camera answers:

**“How should it feel right now?”**

Rules:
- Never aggressive rotations.
- Prefer slow pushes, elegant orbital arcs, subtle vertical drift.
- Camera speed changes must align with emotional intensity.
- Motion is guided by story beats: mystery, conflict, transformation, reveal.

---

## 2. Virtual Lens Philosophy
*(Implementation later maps to actual lens/fov; for now define the cinematic intent.)*

### 2.1 Default lens range
- Wide “world-establishing”: 18–28mm feel
- Mid “identity & architecture”: 28–40mm feel
- Hero “monumental typography”: 40–70mm feel (compression)
- Close “climax reveals”: 70–100mm feel

### 2.2 Lens behavior rules
- Wide shots = reveal scale and depth.
- Mid shots = clarity of architecture systems.
- Close-ups = emotional emphasis (signature object, glowing clause, triage threshold).

---

## 3. Depth of Field (DOF) & Focus Pulls
DOF must feel physically plausible.

Rules:
- Mystery: deeper DOF than conflict (more environment readable, less contrast).
- Conflict: shallower DOF to isolate failure points.
- Reveal: snap into clarity (focus resolves) without hard cuts.

Focus targets:
- Signature object
- Emerging title typography during climax
- One “meaning element” per beat (glowing clause, triage threshold, deterministic grading nucleus)

---

## 4. Camera Movement Language (Shot Types)
Define shot types and when to use them.

### 4.1 Tracking Shot (Follow)
- Camera moves parallel to world geometry.
- Used when the story is “approaching understanding.”
- Speed: slow. No shake.

### 4.2 Push-in (Discovery Intensifier)
- Camera slowly moves toward hero subject.
- Used right before the emotional reveal (title climax).

### 4.3 Pull-out (Release / Transition)
- Camera slowly retreats.
- Used as fog absorbs the world into transition.

### 4.4 Orbital Motion (Elegant orbital arc)
- Camera circles signature object.
- Used for “completion” moments and ending silence.

### 4.5 Static Hero Framing
- Camera holds still when typography resolves or when silence is narrative.

### 4.6 Crane-like Lift (Subtle vertical drift)
- Used for mystery → reveal.
- Movement is minimal and intentional.

---

## 5. Camera Speed, Acceleration, Ease Curves (Design rules)
### 5.1 Speed bands
- **Calm:** very slow (breathing moments)
- **Mystery:** slow but with slight forward intent
- **Conflict:** slow with sharper focus changes (not faster motion necessarily)
- **Reveal:** slow push or brief clarity snap while motion remains premium

### 5.2 Ease curve philosophy
- Natural deceleration is mandatory.
- No bouncy or springy camera.
- No linear, mechanical camera glide.

---

## 6. Transition Shots (How cuts work)
PROJECT ZERO transitions must feel like traveling through a set, not switching pages.

### 6.1 Transition camera behaviors
- Camera pushes into fog boundary.
- Foreground parallax shifts slightly faster.
- Signature object acts like a lens absorbing fragments.

### 6.2 Transition emphasis targets
At transition start:
- focus on the “failure/conflict” element (clause fracture, unstable triage path, chaos collision)

At transition end:
- focus resolves into the next world’s hero atmosphere.

---

## 7. Camera Behavior by Emotional State
Map emotion → camera behavior.

### 7.1 Mystery
- Slow forward drift
- Slightly wider lens feel
- Lower contrast lighting
- Focus: environment depth remains readable

### 7.2 Conflict
- Focus isolates the failure point
- Geometry edges sharpen
- Camera still moves slowly but with more decisive targeting
- Fog increases locally

### 7.3 Transformation
- Camera passes through the signature object or a boundary geometry
- Particles drift faster in foreground
- Focus follows the “rebuilding” path

### 7.4 Reveal
- Camera settles
- Typography emergence is the hero
- Clarity snap: DOF resolves smoothly
- Light bloom controlled but noticeable

### 7.5 Resolution / Calm
- Camera holds or gently orbits
- Fog is minimal
- Quiet particles drift

---

## 8. Shot List (Global)
These are reusable across scenes.

1. **Wide Establishing:** show world scale and fog boundary
2. **Approach:** subtle push-in to emotion element
3. **Isolation:** focus pull to one failure/reconstruction component
4. **Reveal Hold:** static or micro-orbit while typography resolves
5. **Dissolve Exit:** pull-out + signature absorption
6. **Next World Entrance:** focus begins blurred, resolves as camera settles

---

## 9. Signature Object Cinematography Rules
During signature object interactions:
- Signature object must receive the brightest controlled emission (not full screen)
- Camera either:
  - orbits slowly for completion
  - or passes behind it to imply absorption/rebuild
- DOF should briefly favor the signature object as the transition anchor.

---

## 10. Validation Checklist (Cinematography)
For each scene shot set:
- Is the camera motion slow and intentional?
- Does focus pull match emotion?
- Is DOF used to communicate narrative priority?
- Does transition camera feel like travel?
- Is the signature object cinematographically emphasized?
