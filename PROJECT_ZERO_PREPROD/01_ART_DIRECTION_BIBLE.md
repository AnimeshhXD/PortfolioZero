# PROJECT ZERO — ART DIRECTION BIBLE (ART DIRECTION DOCUMENT 1)

> **Status:** Pre-Production (Planning Only) — **No code.**

---

## 0. Document Intent
This Bible defines the complete, internal artistic language of **PROJECT ZERO** so that every scene remains emotionally coherent while still feeling like a handcrafted film set.

It is used to eliminate design inconsistency before implementation.

---

## 1. Visual Philosophy
### 1.1 Visitor Emotional Target
The visitor must feel:
- **Immersion**: not browsing, not scrolling pages.
- **Intimacy**: the experience feels personal and authored.
- **Confidence**: engineering portrayed as calm, intentional craft.
- **Curiosity**: mystery increases before information appears.
- **Weight**: typography and objects feel physically present.
- **Breath**: moments of silence and negative space are intentional.

### 1.2 Storytelling Through Environment
Information is delivered as *observations*:
- Motion reveals systems.
- Lighting explains consequences.
- Depth and fog separate layers of narrative.
- Transitions transform the *world*, not just the layout.

### 1.3 Core Aesthetic Keywords
- **Premium**
- **Minimal**
- **Dark**
- **Elegant**
- **AAA game opening cinematic**
- **Apple-like restraint**
- **Timeless, not trendy**

### 1.4 What This Project Refuses
These are forbidden “portfolio vibes”:
- generic hero/sections
- cards and grids
- stock neon cyberpunk
- “typing terminal” effects
- green terminal UI / matrix rain
- developer dashboard aesthetics
- contribution graphs / skill bars / statistics walls

## 2. Shape Language
Shape language is the visual grammar that makes the world recognizable across projects.

### 2.1 Allowed Primary Forms
1. **Monoliths (silenced geometry)**
   - Tall, heavy forms with softened edges.
   - Used to communicate stability and “core systems.”
2. **Floating architectural frames**
   - Planes, struts, arches, and scaffolds.
   - Suggests structure without “UI.”
3. **Broken geometry / fracture planes**
   - Used during conflict.
   - Edges glow softly; pieces reassemble later.
4. **Organic structures**
   - Neural pathways (abstract tubes/threads).
   - Swarm-like microforms for particles.
5. **Curved volumes**
   - Domes, throats, lens shapes.
   - Used for transitions and “memory/AI” moments.

### 2.2 Curves vs Sharp Edges (Emotion Mapping)
- **Curves dominate**: calm, synthesis, guidance.
- **Sharp edges dominate**: conflict, errors, timeouts, constraints.
- **Hybrid transitions**: during reveals, fractures soften into curves.

### 2.3 Negative Space Philosophy
Negative space is not emptiness—it’s *cinematic pressure*.
Rules:
- Avoid filling the screen with UI noise.
- Typography and hero objects occupy clear focal zones.
- Particles exist in depth; never as 2D overlays.

### 2.4 Negative Space Zones (Composition Controls)
Define 5 persistent zones for framing:
- **Zone A (Hero Zone):** where monumental typography lives.
- **Zone B (Atmosphere Zone):** fog density and light rays.
- **Zone C (World Geometry):** monoliths/structures.
- **Zone D (Interaction Field):** mouse-influenced particle drift.
- **Zone E (Silence Margin):** spare space for breathing moments.

### 2.5 Signature Object Shape Language (Recurring Identity)
A single **Signature Object** must recur throughout the entire experience and evolve after each project.

Choose **one** in implementation; until then, define constraints:
- Must be recognizable at a glance (silhouette identity).
- Must be able to shift materials/lighting/emission without changing the silhouette.
- Must be capable of absorbing/transforming worlds during transitions.
- It must live in all major transitions (scene-to-scene).

**Candidate shape archetypes** (choose one later):
- luminous crystal (faceted prism)
- neural core monolith (organic-to-machined evolution)
- glass monolith (architectural, reflective)
- energy seed (spherical/seed-like)

## 3. Material Library
Materials create realism of emotion. Each material must specify:
- **Use cases**
- **Never use cases**
- Lighting response expectations (how it reflects, emits, fog interacts)

### 3.1 Glass
**Appearance:** translucent, layered, reflective; never “cheap glass.”
- Use:
  - identity calm moments
  - reveals (architecture diagrams “floating”)
  - foreground layer depth
- Never use:
  - ubiquitous glass overlays (forbidden “glass everywhere”)
  - neon glass outlines

Glass lighting behavior:
- Subtle internal scattering.
- Specular highlights should move with camera, not wobble randomly.

### 3.2 Metal
**Appearance:** brushed steel, titanium, dark alloy.
- Use:
  - system stability
  - Mavira OS security barriers
  - workplace ordering “protocol metal”
- Never use:
  - chrome mirror blast
  - loud specular flicker

Metal lighting behavior:
- Soft anisotropic reflections.
- Shadows with gentle penumbra.

### 3.3 Stone
**Appearance:** matte, weighty, quiet.
- Use:
  - narrative gravitas
  - conflict “heaviness”
  - ending closure
- Never use:
  - sand-texture randomness

Stone lighting behavior:
- Low specular, high diffusion.

### 3.4 Fog (Volumetric Material)
Fog is not background. It is a **narrative boundary**.
- Use:
  - dissolving architecture
  - revealing the next scene behind a fog curtain
- Never use:
  - flat haze covering the whole scene equally

Fog interaction rules:
- Fog density changes with emotional state.
- Fog must respect depth ordering (foreground fog vs background fog).

### 3.5 Particles
**Appearance:** tiny points + micro-sparks + volumetric motes.
- Use:
  - calm ambiance
  - neural pathway visualization
  - fracture-to-assemble transitions
- Never use:
  - fireworks
  - fast chaotic particle storms

Particle lighting rules:
- Particles respond to mouse lighting as subtle intensity shifts.
- Particles should not look like 2D sprites with harsh edges.

### 3.6 Liquid
**Appearance:** calm liquid light, slow-moving volume.
- Use:
  - memory compression (Personal Health Coach)
  - discovery flows (AI synthesis)
- Never use:
  - viscous goo overload

### 3.7 Light (Emission Materials)
Light is treated as a material.
- Use:
  - portal reveals
  - “lines draw” moments
  - architecture diagram illumination
- Never use:
  - full-screen glow that harms readability

Light behavior:
- Soft bloom, controlled intensity.
- Use god rays sparingly; only in discovery/emotional peaks.

### 3.8 Energy
**Appearance:** neural threads, subtle heat shimmer.
- Use:
  - AI agents emerging
  - threat/conflict (risk errors)
  - deterministic constraints (rails)
- Never use:
  - overstated neon arcs

Energy behavior:
- Energy respects physical plausibility: thickness, fade, attenuation.

## 4. Lighting Bible
Lighting is the emotional director.

### 4.1 Lighting Philosophy
- **Key light** creates form.
- **Fill light** preserves readability.
- **Rim light** separates foreground from background.
- **Ambient light** sets mood.
- **Volumetric lighting** creates atmosphere and depth.

Never use lighting as decoration. Every highlight must justify the shot.

### 4.2 Key Light
- Source types:
  - hidden practicals embedded in architecture
  - signature object emission
- Behavior:
  - slowly changes direction based on camera movement.
  - mouse affects it subtly (soft lighting, not cursor glow).

### 4.3 Fill Light
- Keep fill restrained to maintain “dark premium.”
- Use fill to clarify typography legibility.

### 4.4 Rim Light
- Rim should enhance silhouette edges.
- Use rim during reveals and transitions.

### 4.5 Ambient Light
- Ambient sets the “weather” of emotion.
- Keep it stable during calm scenes.

### 4.6 Volumetric Lighting
- God rays used only during:
  - meaningful reveals
  - peace-after-conflict moments
  - signature object growth climax

### 4.7 Bloom
Bloom must be:
- soft
- physically restrained
- tuned for expensive feel

Forbidden:
- neon bloom outlines
- bloom used to compensate for low contrast

### 4.8 Fog Interaction
Fog must:
- absorb light gradually
- create depth cues
- dissolve objects into atmosphere during scene cuts

Rules:
- As fog density increases, contrast decreases.
- Typography should remain readable by adjusting its lighting/ink resolution (not by adding UI).

### 4.9 Reflection Rules
- Reflections should be subtle and realistic.
- Glass/metal must not reflect UI text.

### 4.10 Shadow Softness
- Prefer soft shadows.
- Shadow softness varies by scene emotion:
  - chaos: sharper, harsher hints
  - resolution: softer, calmer shadows

### 4.11 Light Animation
All light animations are slow and purposeful:
- pulse = rare and significant
- sweep = used during transitions and architecture drawing
- shimmer = only for particles or liquid volumes

### 4.12 Project-Specific Lighting Behavior (Emotion States)
Define emotional states (shared across projects):
- **Hope / Discovery**: cool blue accents, gentle god rays
- **Isolation**: dark interior lighting, low ambient, more fog
- **Chaos**: fragmented light directions, unstable intensity
- **Stability**: consistent key light, calmer shadows
- **Confidence**: crisp rim separation, balanced bloom

Mapping examples:
- LawLens-X: scanning beams + cyan-violet key light
- Triage Assistant: tri-color zones (green/yellow/red) as volumetric lighting regions
- Workplace Agent: protocol lighting—aligned beams
- BenefitBridge: civic glow; connections illuminate slowly
- Mavira OS: security barriers—harder light edges but stable
- Personal Health Coach: warm calm glow + rose diffusion

## 5. Color Bible
Color must feel expensive and controlled.

### 5.1 Global Palette (Neutral Stage)
Base neutrals (global):
- Near-black graphite: `#07080C` to `#0A0B10`
- Smoke gray: `#1B1C22`
- Soft slate: `#2A2C35`
- UI glass gray: `rgba(255,255,255,0.06)`

### 5.2 Accent Palette (Global Accent Families)
Choose accents per project; global families:
- Cyan / Deep Blue (AI/neural)
- Violet / Orchid (systems/architecture)
- Emerald (impact/operations)
- Amber (security/governance)
- Rose (calm wellness)

### 5.3 Project Palettes (Assigned)
- **LawLens-X:** deep cyan + violet
- **Triage Assistant:** emerald + amber + red thresholds
- **Workplace Operations Agent:** violet + electric blue
- **BenefitBridge:** emerald + amber civic glow
- **Mavira OS:** steel + cyan (cool stability)
- **Personal Health Coach:** rose + deep cyan (warm peace)

### 5.4 Gradient Rules
- Gradients must support depth: they should shift with fog density.
- Gradients are used in:
  - sky background
  - subtle ambient illumination

Forbidden:
- rainbow gradients
- aggressive high saturation overlays

### 5.5 Contrast Rules
- Typography must remain readable at all times.
- If contrast drops due to fog, increase illumination *on typography materials* rather than UI overlays.

### 5.6 Accessibility Rules (Visual Accessibility)
- Provide equivalent accessibility via contrast and luminous resolution.
- Reduced motion mode must not rely on color-only state (e.g., triage must include shape/threshold cues).

### 5.7 Forbidden Combinations
- Neon green + magenta simultaneously (cinema but not gaming neon)
- High-saturation colors as primary background fills
- White text over pure black with no intermediate blacks (creates harsh edges)

## 6. Typography Bible
Typography is monumental and architectural.

### 6.1 Primary Type
Primary type (implementation later):
- bold geometric sans with premium spacing behavior.
- Must support extremely large scales without pixel shimmer.

### 6.2 Secondary Type
Secondary type:
- refined alternative weight (or condensed variant).
- Monospace reserved for code-like excerpts.

### 6.3 Monospace
Monospace used only in:
- architecture snippets moments
- brief technology reveals

Avoid “ASCII terminal vibe.”

### 6.4 Hierarchy
Levels:
- **L1:** Scene titles (extra-large, cinematic)
- **L2:** Subtitles / roles (medium)
- **L3:** Micro labels (portals: Resume, GitHub, LinkedIn, Email)
- **L4:** Code excerpt characters (monospace, subtle)

### 6.5 Spacing / Kerning / Tracking
- Large titles: slightly negative or neutral tracking for weight.
- Subtitles: wider tracking to feel calm and premium.
- Avoid overspacing that makes text feel like a poster template.

### 6.6 Motion (Typography Animation)
Typography must not slide in.
Allowed motion behaviors:
- **Emergence from fog** (letters materialize with depth blur to sharpness)
- **Light pass** (a sweep of illumination across letters)
- **Ink resolution** (opacity + blur convergence)

Forbidden:
- generic fade only (sometimes okay, but not the default)
- jittering type
- typing effects

### 6.7 Opacity / Depth / Scale ratios
- Titles must maintain stable scale once resolved.
- During transitions, text can:
  - dissolve into particles
  - be absorbed by signature object glow

### 6.8 “Never use typography as decoration”
Typography must carry meaning:
- It is the shot anchor.
- Its motion equals narrative emphasis.

## 7. Composition Bible
### 7.1 Rule of Thirds (Cinematic variation)
Use the third-rule as a starting point, then break it deliberately when emotion demands.

### 7.2 Negative Space
- Never crowd the screen.
- Maintain a consistent silence margin.

### 7.3 Eye Guidance
- Direct attention via:
  - light rays
  - rim highlights
  - particle motion directions
  - architecture lines drawing

### 7.4 Reading Direction
Even in a cinematic experience, the visitor must perceive order:
- Scene title first (resolved)
- then supporting role lines
- later project reveal

### 7.5 Balance
Balance between:
- typography weight
- geometry mass
- fog softness
- particle density

### 7.6 Framing & Focus
- Use shallow depth-of-field for hero moments.
- Use focus pull during reveals.

### 7.7 Focus during Emotional States
- Mystery: deeper focus but low contrast
- Conflict: sharper contrast edges, tighter focus
- Resolution: softened focus, calmer lighting

## 8. Motion Philosophy
Everything must feel expensive.

### 8.1 Tempo Rules
- Nothing moves quickly.
- Motion ranges should be constrained:
  - camera: slow push-ins/pull-outs
  - objects: gentle drift
  - transitions: measured with breathing moments

### 8.2 No Random Spins
Forbidden motion patterns:
- random spinning cubes
- chaotic physics bounces
- “freeform” motion without narrative purpose

Allowed patterns:
- orbital drift (elegant orbital motion)
- linear or eased camera pushes
- deliberate assembly / disassembly

### 8.3 Easing Rules
- Premium easing: natural deceleration.
- Avoid overly elastic bounces.

### 8.4 Purpose Rule
Every animation has purpose:
- teach a system
- express an emotional state
- emphasize a reveal or transition

### 8.5 Silence as a Motion Tool
Silence is part of the animation timeline.
At least one breathing moment per scene.

## 9. Design Principles (30–50 Immutable Laws)
Below are **40** laws.

1. No portfolio navigation patterns.
2. No cards/generic sections.

4. Typography is architectural, not decorative.
5. Never move the camera without narrative purpose.
6. Camera movement is slow and confident.

8. Particles are volumetric and depth-respecting.
9. Fog is a boundary, not a background overlay.
10. Contrast must preserve legibility.
11. Lighting explains emotion.
12. Accents appear primarily through light/bloom, not neon UI.
13. Use one signature object recurring across all scenes.
14. Signature object evolves after each project.
15. Transition absorbs/rebuilds through signature object.
16. Avoid hard neon outlines.
17. Bloom is restrained and physically plausible.
18. Metal and glass should feel heavy and real.
19. Stone is quiet and stable.
20. No typing effects.
21. No fake terminals.
22. No matrix rain.
23. No GitHub graph aesthetics.
24. No skill bars.
25. No stats walls.
26. No excessive glass overlays.
27. Never reveal project title first.
28. Emotional arc happens before information.
29. Problem must be felt before it is named.
30. Project reveal feels like climax.
31. Screenshots are massive, full-bleed planes, not card thumbnails.
32. Any “UI-like” text must be treated as in-world signage.
33. God rays are rare and meaningful.
34. Shadows are soft unless in conflict.
35. Reduced motion mode must keep story intact.
36. Accessibility state must not rely on color alone.
37. Keep palette consistent; per-project accents vary within allowed families.
38. Negative space is preserved to breathe.
39. If an animation can be removed without losing emotion, remove it.
40. Everything must feel timeless.
